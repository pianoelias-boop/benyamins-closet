## Read of the signals
Written 2026-09-20 from the 97 pieces he saved himself (his spreadsheet of "cool pieces"), before the notebook
has any hearts. The closet itself is the taste until the notebook fills.

- **Legs are wide, high and pleated.** Nearly a third of what he saved is trousers or jeans: wide legs, double
  pleats, high rises, roomy hips, relaxed straights. Fatigue and cargo cuts (M-47, K-pant, jacquard cargo),
  painter pants, hakama pants, paratrooper pants. Never slim. A "reasonable taper" on a relaxed Japanese jean is
  as narrow as it goes.
- **Fabric with a surface.** Herringbone (HBT, linen herringbone, herringbone selvedge), seersucker in
  colours, hickory and ticking stripes, corduroy, waffle and rice stitch, sashiko, patchwork, khadi, open-weave
  wool. Japanese denim (ONI, Full Count, 3sixteen, Studio D'Artisan). He reads fibre lines: linen-cotton denim,
  merino-alpaca-linen, merino terry inside a cotton hoodie all earned a note.
- **Knits with a pattern or a texture.** Mohair cardigans, cable knits (undyed, or an Avatar-coloured one),
  Norwegian and marled colours, a mushroom sweater, fuzzy alpaca with patches, a colour-block cardigan, a
  ribbed knit tee. Boxy and cropped is welcome.
- **Colour:** olive and every green first (chartreuse, forest, dark avocado, kelp), then browns, ecru and
  cream, indigo and midnight teal, navy, Nantucket red, one yellow parka. Earth tones over brights; a bold
  pattern is fine when the ground is natural.
- **Outerwear:** light and characterful. Hunting jackets in seersucker, corduroy ranch and Donegal jackets,
  waxed cotton (coverall, duffle, cropped rain jacket), a shearling-collar ranch coat, a cotton sherpa coach
  jacket, a wool-cashmere trucker. He notes when something "would actually fit the chest".
- **Shoes:** casual and comfortable with a twist. Beefroll penny loafers, beaded boat shoes, kilim loafers, camp
  mocs with a lug sole, hairy suede hikers, side-zip boots; Mephisto and Scarpa for "allegedly super comfy".
- **Shirts:** camp collars, cropped and textured linen, map-print rayon, indigo khadi work shirts, ikat,
  embroidered flannel, patchwork corduroy overshirts.
- **Price:** most saves sit between $100 and $400; he saves $600 to $1,200 coats knowing he will wait for a sale.
  "When on sale and available in my size" is a recurring note.

## Told in person
- 2026-09-20: he is the curator of this closet; every piece so far is his own save, with his note attached.
- Sizing, from his own notes: usually M or L in tops, 32 (sometimes 31 or 34 for the hip) in trousers, 44 in
  tailoring; a roomy hip and thigh matter more than the label size. Some brands he sizes up in and has the
  waist taken in.

## Guidance for rounds
- Lead with wide or pleated trousers and jeans in textured cloth, patterned or textured knitwear, camp-collar
  and work shirts, and light outerwear with character.
- Favour olive and green, brown, ecru, indigo and midnight teal; a bright works as a stripe or a pattern on a
  natural ground.
- Pieces that breathe and move are prized: he dances (Lindy Hop) and travels for work, so linen, seersucker,
  poplin, open-weave wool and lightweight cotton in a wide leg score twice. "Hanging out" means genuinely
  casual (tees, jeans, cargos, sweats); "Going out to eat" means put-together but not dressy (camp shirts,
  good trousers, loafers, cardigans with character); "Dressy" is tailoring, overcoats, penny loafers.
- Rules from the owner: $50–$500 list, at least 70% natural fibre, reputable brands only.
- **Steer clear of slim, skinny and tapered legs** in trousers, jeans and shorts; wide, straight, relaxed and
  pleated legs are what he saves. A relaxed Japanese jean with a gentle taper is the one exception he has noted.
- Womenswear pieces do not belong in a round even from a stockist that carries both.
